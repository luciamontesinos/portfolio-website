During January of 2026 I started the [Genuary Challenge](https://genuary.art/), which consists of creative coding around a prompt a day over the course of 31 days. 

Everything was done using p5.js, and I was particularly focused on having motion in the designs. Below, I explain and show what was behind the ideas, but if you just want to see it, go to [@luciam0ntesin0s on Instagram](https://www.instagram.com/luciam0ntesin0s)


Here is some more info about what I did:

- Day 1. "One Color, one shape". For day 1, I made an animation of a 27-point star, and I was playing with the inner and outer radius sizes and skewing it. Why a star? Because I have a tattoo of three skewed stars with many points. Why do I have a tattoo of a skewed star? Because why not.

- Day 2. "Twelve principles of animation". For day 2, I took the star from day 1 as a starting point, and made a ripple / wavy effect. I didn't really have a clear creative goal, but halfway through, I started picturing the angled stars as scales, and then it made sense to imagine how they would move in the body of a fish.

- Day 3. "Fibonacci Forever". For day 3, I wanted to explore some kind of loop as going through doors (yes, I also have a tattoo of doors) in which the dimensions of the doors corresponded to the Fibonacci sequence. As I was doing that, I started to picture some kind of infinite tunnel and wanted to create a motion effect of going through that tunnel. Then I explored that with colors, eventually dropping the idea of doors or frames and including the floor as part of the tunneling motion. I paired this animation with a Shepard's tone audio.

- Day 4. "Lowres". For day 4, I was exploring Perlin noise to create some kind of fire, just like in a project I did many years ago. However, inspired by day 2, I decided to explore waves and water. For this, I first drew the background of the ocean using a noise function, and based on these values obtained, created shapes for different noise thresholds, making the effect of light reflections and water depth.

- Day 5. "Write genuary, avoid using a font". For day 5, I decided to focus on the counter of the letters, the negative space. Each character was an off-white square, and within each square I drew in blue squares the counter. I chose these colors because they reminded me of tiles. The animation here was a bit inspired by slot machines, with columns moving vertically at different times, which I also extended to rows. The end result is a loop that goes from column movements to stillness, to row movements to stillness.

- Day 6. "Lights on/off". For day 6, I was thinking about what could change when switching a light. The eyes of a creature looking at you. You turn on the lights. It's gone. Well, it was just a cat looking at you, and when you turn on the light you can see their ball bouncing. I paired this animation with the sounds of a light switching, a cat meowing, and a ball bouncing.

- Day 7. "Boolean algebra". For day 7, I took as a starting point the Venn diagrams for the complement operation, and focused on how to animate it. Eventually, it became a grid of balls rolling from different directions until reaching a predetermined one, making a popping noise when doing so.

- Day 8. "A city". For day 8, I decided to work with Copenhagen. I was inspired by some of the [aerial photography by Nicolas Cosedis](https://www.welovecph.com/), which shows the different Copenhagen areas from up above. I took as a reference a photography of [Kartoffelrækkerne](https://www.welovecph.com/%C3%B8sterbroandnordhavn?pgid=lx6ki3dy1-7888ff25-a056-4247-ad6c-5746978e2f03), and noticed how the houses look at first glance mostly the same, with only a few houses popping in color. When inspecting them up close you notice how the roofs have different shapes, different windows, different shades of bricks. With this in mind, I created a set of variables for the height and width of floors, roof and windows, number of windows, shapes for the roof, and color for the roof and bricks. Then I just randomly generated the houses and animated the scene, showing one house at a time. In the end, I decided to use a different color palette instead of the colors from Kartoffelrækkerne. I decided to use the famous Nyhavn houses and their colors as reference.


The main learning from this challenge was to actually sit down and get it started, although I knew the work deadlines were going to catch me and I was not going to be able to finish it. However, in line with the Genuary manifesto, "this is supposed to be fun and creative" and that is exactly what I got out of these days. It also pushed me to finally post on my Instagram account, and connect with some other people working on the challenge.
