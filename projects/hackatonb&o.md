I participated in a 1-day hackaton organised by [The Tech Bros](https://thetechbros.io/) and hosted by [Bang & Olufsen](https://www.bang-olufsen.com/en/dk). The hackaton, titled "ctrl + alt + gesture", brought together 24 women working or studying within STEM, with goal of exploring new ways of interacting with B&O's product range. We were teamed up in groups of three, and after being introduced to the topic, we had around 3-4 hours to get hands on. The result? My team won first place and we got to take home the B&O Beoplay HX! But you are here to read about the process, right?

Given that we had a short time to design, and needed to have a working demo, we decided to focus on a concrete scenario and design around it.  

Let's say you are hosting an event, either a hackaton or a dinner party. You want your participants or guests to know where to go when they arrive, and you want the music to match the energy of the room. Perhaps you want some calmer music when your guest are arriving and meeting each other. Or maybe you want the music to lift up the energy in the room when it's time to party. But over all, what you want the most is to spend time with your guests, without having to worry about all of these details.

What we proposed in our solution is to leverage the existing software and hardware that B&O has and create a feature that would help you run events smoothly. We called it "Space Flow". In the images you can see some wireframes we quickly mocked up based on their current app. On top of their app, we added a feature that allows you to create an event around the different spaces in the hosting venue and configure it around the events agenda, which you can synchronise with your calendar.

Moreover, we incorporated actions, which are sound events that trigger changes in the playback of the sound. This works using an audio feature classifier. With this, we are able to detect when someone is giving a speech, by simply recognising when someone clicks on a glass multiple times; and when the speech is over as we recognise the applause. By detecting these events, we can 
 stop the music when the speech is about to begin and then bring the sound back once the speech is over. Simple, but effective.

I really enjoyed being part of this event. Not only was it an interesting challenge, but it brought together young women working in different STEM fields, creating a valuable opportunity to network.

In this project, I was in charge of the wireframes, done in [Figma](https://www.figma.com/) and the working demo, done in [Max/MSP](https://cycling74.com/products/max).
