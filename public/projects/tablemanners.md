Table Manners is the third and last artefact developed in my PhD, exploring how to lower the stakes of taking part in music-making, with participants' voices as the sonic material. It consists of a set of dinnerware objects made of cardboard and papier-mâché, scaled up to about five times their normal size. Participants are invited to grab and strike the objects, to assign a sound to each object combination, and then to voice that sound with each hit. 

## Research

Table Manners facilitates participatory music-making by providing a playful context that does not necessarily read as musical at first, lowering the expectations that usually come with making music. The absurd premise of banging and making noises with comically large, unpolished objects signals that what happens does not need to be taken seriously. The objects suggest gestures without prescribing a correct way of handling them, and their scale calls for the whole body and invites several people to interact together. The same holds for sound: the objects may suggest some, but there is no correct sound to assign to each one and no vocal skill to live up to.

Four participants tried out Table Manners in a proof-of-concept play test in a lab setting.
